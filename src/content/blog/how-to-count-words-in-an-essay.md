To count the words in my essay correctly, I need two things: a counter, and the rule for what is included. The second matters more.

## Find the rule first

Check the assignment brief or ask. Common questions:

- Do the title, headings and subheadings count?
- Are in-text citations included?
- Is the reference list or bibliography excluded?
- What about footnotes, captions, tables and appendices?
- Is there a tolerance, or is the limit strict?

Write the answers down before you edit.

## Count only the parts that count

1. Copy the body text, leaving out the excluded sections.
2. Paste it into the [Word Counter](/word-counter/). It counts words, characters, sentences and paragraphs as you type, and the text stays in your browser.
3. Note the total, then check any section limits separately.

![The word counter showing totals for words, characters, sentences and paragraphs](/blog-images/how-to-count-words-in-an-essay-example.jpg "Count the sections your rule includes, not the whole document by default.")

## Why two counters disagree

Programs make different decisions about hyphenated terms, numbers, symbols, web addresses and dashes without spaces. Small differences are normal. If your institution names a tool or checks submissions in a particular program, treat that count as the one that matters and leave a small margin.

Text copied from a PDF can also arrive with words split across lines. Check for broken words before counting.

## Pages are only an estimate

In a common 12-point font with standard margins, 1,000 words is roughly two single-spaced pages or four double-spaced pages. A different font, spacing, headings, quotations and images change the page count considerably.

## If you are over the limit

- Remove points that repeat what you already showed.
- Cut openings such as "It is important to note that".
- Replace long phrases with one word.
- Shorten quotations to the words you actually analyze.
- Do not move essential argument into footnotes or appendices to avoid the count; that may break the rules.

## If you are under

Look for a claim without evidence, an example you did not explain, or a counter-argument you skipped. Adding filler is easy to spot and weakens the essay.

For platforms that limit **characters** instead of words, use the [Character Counter](/character-counter/). To estimate a spoken presentation, the [reading time calculator](/reading-time-calculator/) converts words into minutes.
