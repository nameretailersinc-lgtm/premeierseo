HEIC is the file type many iPhones use for photos. It saves space, but some websites, upload forms and older software will not accept it. You can convert a HEIC file to JPG, or have your phone take JPEG photos from now on.

## Stop an iPhone creating HEIC photos

Apple's instructions are:

1. Open **Settings**, then tap **Camera**.
2. Tap **Formats**, then tap **Most Compatible**.

New photos and videos then use JPEG or H.264. The option appears only on devices that can capture in the newer formats. It does not convert photos you have already taken. Apple also notes that photos may be converted automatically when you share or import them to a device that does not support HEIF.

## Convert on a Mac with Preview

1. Open the photo in **Preview**.
2. Choose **File > Export**.
3. In the **Format** menu choose **JPEG** and set the quality.
4. Name the file, choose where to save it, and click **Save**.

Preview can export several images at once if you open them in one window and select them all in the sidebar.

## Convert on Windows, Android or any browser

Whether Windows can open HEIC photos depends on the codecs installed. Microsoft distributes HEIF and HEVC extensions through the Microsoft Store, and one of them may carry a charge. Converting avoids installing anything:

1. Open the [HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg-converter/).
2. Choose one or more .heic or .heif photos.
3. Set the JPG quality.
4. Tick **Keep photo details** if you want the date taken, camera and location to stay in the file.
5. Download each photo, or all of them as a ZIP.

![The HEIC to JPG converter before files are added, showing its quality and photo detail options](/blog-images/how-to-convert-heic-to-jpg-example.jpg "Choose the quality and whether to keep photo details before you convert.")

The conversion happens in your browser. Outside Safari, a decoder downloads the first time you convert, and large photos can take a few seconds each. Some Live Photo, burst or edited files may not decode; export those as JPG from the Photos app.

## Check location data before sharing

Photos can record where they were taken. Leave **Keep photo details** off if you do not want to pass that on, and check the converted file's information if privacy matters.

## After converting

A JPG can be larger than the HEIC it came from, because the formats compress differently. If a form has a size limit, follow [how to reduce image size in KB](/blog/reduce-photo-size-in-kb/). Keep your HEIC originals until you have checked the copies.
