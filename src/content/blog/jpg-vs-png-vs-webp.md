Choosing a JPG, PNG or WebP format comes down to two questions: what is in the picture, and what will open it.

## The short answer

![Comparison table of JPG, PNG and WebP: compression type, transparency, animation and best use](/blog-images/jpg-vs-png-vs-webp-example.jpg "What each format supports and where it fits.")

| | JPG | PNG | WebP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compression | Lossy | Lossless | Lossy or lossless |
| Transparency | No | Yes | Yes |
| Animation | No | Not in standard PNG | Yes |
| Typical use | Photographs | Logos, screenshots, line art | Web pages |

## When JPG is the right choice

JPG removes detail that is hard to notice in photographs, so photo files become much smaller. It is accepted almost everywhere, which matters for forms and email. It is a poor choice for sharp text and flat colors, where it leaves visible smudges around edges. It also cannot store transparency.

## When PNG is the right choice

PNG keeps every pixel exactly. Use it for logos, interface screenshots, diagrams and anything that needs a transparent background. The cost is size: a photograph saved as PNG is usually far larger than the same photograph as a good-quality JPG.

## When WebP is the right choice

WebP covers both jobs, with lossy and lossless modes and transparency. It often produces smaller files than the older formats at similar visible quality, but results depend on the image and settings, so compare your own files.

## What happens when you convert

- **PNG to JPG:** transparent areas must be filled with a color. [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg-converter/) lets you choose white, black or a custom background.
- **JPG to PNG:** [JPG to PNG](/jpg-to-png-converter/) stops further loss in later edits, but does not restore detail and usually makes the file bigger.
- **Anything to a lossy format:** each new lossy save can lose a little more. Convert from the original, not from an earlier copy.

Changing the file extension is not conversion. The data stays in the old format, and some programs will reject the file.

## A quick way to decide

1. Is it a photograph going into a form or email? Use JPG.
2. Does it need a transparent background? Use PNG, or WebP if the destination accepts it.
3. Is it text, a screenshot or a logo? Use PNG or lossless WebP.
4. Is it for your own website? Test WebP and compare size and appearance.
